# Fragony maps

All five maps rotate automatically from a persistent shuffled bag. Starting a new run or advancing a round draws one map; every map appears before the bag refills, and the next bag avoids immediately repeating the last map. New runs do not reset the bag. Continuing an active match keeps its map; continuing the shop waits until the next round to draw. The bag is stored separately from the run so it survives losses, new runs and reloads. Legacy saves count their resumed map as already seen when initializing the rotation. There is no manual map selector. `tests/map-rotation.mjs` verifies bag cycles, boundary repeats, new runs, saves/continues, malformed state and legacy migration.

## Test Works
Original three-tier arena. Recovery now has three four-item 5 HP trails, five 25 HP packs, and one central 50 HP overhealth reward. Small trails mark movement through the Press rim, Range bridge and Assembly return rather than concentrating every resource at the center.

## Reservoir
Geometry conversion of the player-supplied `ctf_reservoir.pk3`, the Elite Force II remake credited to **[SFI]Valix** in its readme. The original Elite Force I layout and other material remain credited to their respective creators. This project retains the supplied architectural geometry and source pickup/spawn coordinates, with Fragony materials and gameplay. Ten additional free-for-all spawns cover the galleries, central crossings and lower reservoir routes. Shared player/bot spawning weighs enemy proximity, sightlines, nearby traffic and recent use of each region. No original game textures, character/item models, sounds, scripts or lightmaps are included.

The disconnected specialty loadout rooms and remote skybox are excluded. The main room connections, curved architecture, stairs, galleries, reservoirs and submerged passages are retained at 32 source units per metre. Four automatic elevator platforms replace the two paired base ladder zones and two central ladder zones. Approach either landing to call a lift; boarding sends it to the opposite floor. Jump swims upward and crouch swims downward. There is no drowning timer.

Bots use spatially distinct patrol destinations, avoid recently visited/crowded routes and relocate during prolonged local fights while retaining combat perception. Height preference is relative and capped so the upper bases do not dominate patrol choices. The ambient-occlusion depth/normal pass renders both sides of BSP surfaces to match the visible walls and prevent hidden geometry appearing as phantom shadows.

All 26 small-health and eight small-armor source placements become 5 HP pickups; the two medium-armor positions become 25 HP. Six additional 25 HP packs support lower landings, galleries and reservoir routes. The single 50 HP reward sits on the exposed central lower crossing. The run keeps its selected map, including across saves.

## Tidal Lock

Original symmetric waterworks, informed by Reservoir’s opposing territories and layered crossings. Azure and Ochre pump houses frame a diagonal central bridge with the single 50 HP reward. Opposing upper transfers lead to exposed arrivals in the other half; the lower channel passes beneath the bridge and around its island. Mid-channel exit ramps and end staircases give the water route different departures. The pump masses, firing windows, interrupted front walls and lower cover divide observation from access. Recovery behind each pump house and on each gallery requires leaving the central collection point. Room signs, cool/warm territory accents, deck-edge bands, floor joints, canopy ribs and suspended trusses provide orientation within the game’s material style.

Twelve FFA starts, two physical elevators, two principal gallery stairs, two end water stairs and two sloped water exits. Twenty 5 HP fragments in four lines, six 25 HP recoveries and one 50 HP major. Geometry and corresponding ordinary resources have 180-degree rotational symmetry. The native water controls apply: crouch/swim down to take the submerged bridge passage; swim up/jump to surface. Walking up the exit ramps works without jumping.

## Parallax

Original symmetric tower/core arena, informed by the custom Voyager 2’s directed access and return cycles. Ion and Arc territories each have a lower room, gallery and screened upper tower. A paired launch transfers the player to the opposite half of the elevated core; drops rejoin the lower fight and independent room returns. The major is under the central spine, so owning the upper crossing does not collect it automatically. Side firing windows reveal circulation but their solid sills block ordinary standing movement. Lower recovery pockets, arrival recovery and tower recovery require different commitments.

The jump pads are a new interpretation of rapid redistribution using Fragony’s existing mechanics; they do not reproduce the source teleporter’s timing or behavior. Twelve starts, two launches, two tower elevators and two gallery staircases; twenty-eight 5 HP fragments, six 25 HP recoveries and one 50 HP major. Three usable floor levels. Collision geometry and corresponding resources retain 180-degree rotational symmetry; territory accents differ to preserve orientation.

## Foundry

Original asymmetric three-room arena: Forge uses a large chimney and angled machine wing; Press combines a cylinder, gallery, firing positions and the lift; Dispatch provides an interrupted outer return and the launch to the contested gantry. The major sits on that gantry rather than the ground. The north heat exchanger blocks a broad cross-map sightline and creates separate approaches to the north service route. Control is a small high room with a restricted view into Press, reached by the service staircase or a launch from a screened side dock. Its arrival opening provides a second point of attack. The dock is off the normal return path to avoid involuntary repeated launches.

Twelve starts, one elevator, two launches, three staircases and three floor levels. Twenty-four 5 HP fragments in five lines, four 25 HP recoveries and one 50 HP major. Warm/cool room masses, room labels, steel plinths, roof canopies and gantry markings distinguish spaces without importing source art.

### Original-map validation and criticism

The maps are part of the normal automatic rotation and saved-map flow, using the current production AI unchanged. `npm run test:maps` checks supported/clear starts and resources, all 144 directed spawn pairs per map, window firing versus standing collision, protected recovery, symmetry where intended, alternate approaches and returns with either tested primary portal blocked, 402 physical bot-controller trips, all special links, four actual player launch landings, swimming under the bridge and walking from water up the exit ramp. The tested strategic viewpoints see at most 8/12, 6/12 and 7/12 starts respectively; this is an authored-viewpoint check, not an exhaustive guarantee against spawn pressure. The major cannot watch every recovery on any of the maps. Runtime navigation builds locally in roughly 0.2–0.5 seconds per map in the geometry checks.

Iteration addressed concrete failures: upper circulation severed by partitions; pickups intersecting geometry; launch headroom below a gallery; lift/stair overlap; drops caught on narrow wall lips; slow distant water exits; cover projecting over an exit ramp; a landing overlapping the unfinished rise of that ramp; a launch accidentally lying on a main return route; a beam clipping the player’s launch arc; and Foundry’s initial 9/12-spawn north-core sightline. The final water exit and launch paths are tested with the actual player controller as well as bot navigation. Presentation was inspected at player height and during native play; the ordinary regression suite passes.

Match observations in `research/maps` separate initial designs, intermediate revisions and final source versions. Native source hashes stop a cohort if implementation changes during it; interrupted or earlier layouts are not pooled as final-map evidence. Current-AI snapshots include actual damage, pickup gains, life identities, regional traffic and goals, upper-floor use and special traversal. Higher-difficulty checks set round-eight AI difficulty after starting a match; they are not simulations of an eight-round purchase history. `npm run test:map-matches` reruns the current-map match observer. `tools/prepare-arena-review.mjs` generates a temporary native-game inspection entry for development.

The final source version has twelve90-second matches: seeds17/81 at initial difficulty and209/375 at higher difficulty, four cases per map. All recorded shots, deaths and collections reconcile with native counters; all living sampled positions are finite and above the world floor; native stuck-recovery counts are zero. Major collections overlap opposing damage in10/11 Tidal Lock collections,11/11 Parallax collections and9/12 Foundry collections. Parallax uses71 launches across its four cases, Foundry36. The water region receives actual opposing damage in all four final Tidal cases. These are diagnostic bot observations with six autonomous actors and no stationary human target, not player probabilities or balance ratings. Earlier intermediate source versions are excluded from these totals. `tools/audit-originals.mjs` verifies current source hashes and counter reconciliation.

Remaining criticism should focus on human route choice and orientation, the exposed central crossings, whether tower/control access earns its travel time, the predictability of launch arrivals and how worthwhile the submerged route feels. Water and the highest rooms see less bot traffic than the public middle; those routes are physically usable, but their human tactical value needs judgment in actual play. These are complete local maps for critical playtesting, not a claim that bot counters establish human balance.

## Health
- 5 HP: small pale-green crosses, can exceed spawn health.
- 25 HP: white packs, capped at spawn health (including Juggernaut).
- 50 HP: one large acid-colored pack per map, can exceed spawn health.
- Overhealth cap: 200 HP. No decay. Small/standard respawn in 15 seconds; major in 30 seconds. Full or ineligible pickups remain available. Player and bots use the same rules. Any actual healing clears damage/assist history as before.

## Rebuilding Reservoir
From the repository root:

```sh
python3 tools/import-reservoir.py /path/to/ctf_reservoir.pk3
npm run bake:reservoir
npm test
```

The importer reads EF2! v20 planes, convex brushes, planar faces and quadratic patches. The primary format reference is Spearmint's `code/qcommon/bsp_ef2.c` (https://github.com/zturtleman/spearmint/blob/master/code/qcommon/bsp_ef2.c); loader implementation was not copied. Visible ladder rungs are removed with their interaction volumes. Rendering keeps material batches; spatial ray-test leaves preserve hit geometry without scanning the entire map per shot. The packed navigation graph includes lift and swimming links and records a geometry/route fingerprint checked by the tests. Run `npm run test:reservoir-flow` for multi-seed match coverage and combat checks across both bases, galleries, central crossings and water routes.

Water presentation uses one clipped surface per original swim volume, sparse moving highlights and clear grazing-angle tint. Water is excluded from the AO depth pass. Underwater fog retains long sightlines; no camera distortion or blur. Swim motion integrates entry velocity, drag and buoyancy instead of overwriting velocity; Space ascends and crouch descends. All four lift shafts have matching visible/collidable catch floors 0.30 m below the lower landing.


## Lessons from the Elite Force spatial investigation

The first slice atlas was a preliminary structural survey and cannot establish flow. Subsequent work reconstructed all 19 source maps with their patches and both custom BSPs, inspected spaces at player height, read entity links and moving-platform parameters, decoded the supplied AAS graphs, and stepped representative routes through collision. The reconstruction is an analysis tool; it does not emulate the original game’s shader rules, physics or mover scheduling.

The references support different circulation models. Noon combines an open shared arena with arches, stairs and a surrounding upper circuit. Raven divides a largely flat layout into rooms and turns that conceal other starts. Voyager 2 connects stacked local routes through doors and four platforms with explicitly authored 280-unit journeys; freezing doors/platforms changes apparent connectivity dramatically. Reverse Voyager’s compiled ordinary start-to-flag and flag-to-flag routes depend on teleport access, with drops returning the carrier to the lower circulation. Temple’s extreme-height rewards are a linked teleporter excursion with a return, rather than ordinary upper-floor control. Reservoir provides a different example: visible layers and distant galleries whose actual access to the lower major is more expensive than proximity in a plan suggests. Do not let Reservoir’s particular form become the default template.

For new Fragony maps, define and physically verify a fight, an escape, a pursuit/counter-route, and the way each rejoins the map. Distinguish walking, drops, stairs, lifts and any special links in a directed graph. Two doors watched from one position are not automatically two tactical approaches. A route can reveal a fight without reaching it quickly, or reach it while concealing its approach. Check sightlines and travel separately. Upper routes should change relationships to lower combat, not simply add elevated floor area. Recognizable room volumes, thresholds and major obstructions should establish orientation and encounter rhythm before small cover props.

Keep the user’s exact three-pickup economy. A 5 HP fragment line can reward a tactically useful rotation and build overheal; ten fragments equal the major’s nominal 50 HP, so evaluate the whole circuit’s collection time, safety and availability. A 25 HP recovery branch should break sightlines and rejoin useful circulation; healthy players need tactical reasons to visit because it cannot overheal. Put exactly one 50 HP overheal pickup where approaches can actually contest collection and departure. Neither a central coordinate nor a contested label proves actual contention. Separate safe sustain, dominant sightlines and the major’s collection point.

Validation must distinguish route planning from physical completion and from observed combat. The stock-map body proxy completed 35 of 38 selected origin-to-origin cases after alternate-route checks; three remain unresolved around physical transitions, and two Raven starts remain disconnected under the inferred clip geometry. These are limits of this reconstruction/controller evidence, not conclusions that the original maps are broken. Original shader semantics are missing. The 21 Reservoir route probes use the actual current game controller; the 3 full-match observations use the current AI, not human play. Special links in stock proxy trials are explicit transitions, not physically simulated original lift rides. Do not quote proxy times as original-game timings.

Local reproducible investigation tools are in research/flow; generated geometry, navigation and observation caches are ignored by Git. The preliminary atlas is retained as source reference, not a completed flow analysis.


### Encounter telemetry and what it changes

The follow-up observer records life identities, damage, deaths, individual effective health gains, pickup availability, goals, lift states and 5 Hz actor/contact snapshots. Potential eye contact is distinguished from plausible direct-fire threat (FOV, current weapon range and spawn grace), actual damage, and successful escape. A clean escape requires three continuous seconds without the original living threat’s LOS or opponent damage; reacquisition resets the clock. Collection contention uses actual opposing damage within three seconds before/after collection, excludes self damage, and retains window censoring. Contact windows ending through occlusion, range, death and round end remain separate. The observer was checked against a genuinely uninstrumented same-seed match: native counters, final actors, positions, health and pickup timers were identical. Recorded damage/deaths, shots and collections reconcile with native game counters; life identity, self damage and escape reset/censor rules have controlled checks.

Twelve 90-second research matches compared Test Works and Reservoir, three seeds each, with the major available or disabled. Both conditions start with identical six autonomous combatants, positions and loadouts; the stationary player is removed and a sixth rusher replaces it. The major-disabled condition is only a research intervention, not a game change. In the Test Works cases, pooled actor time within 10 m of the major fell from 218.6 to 115.4 actor-seconds and nearby damage fell from 1017 to 320 when it was disabled, while total deaths stayed 27. Nearby damage fell in all three seeds. This supports a location/traffic role for the major in these bot trials; total kills alone would miss it. Five of six Test Works major collections overlapped opposing damage within the defined window, versus three of six in Reservoir. Three seeds and bots do not establish human balance.

A controlled test of the actual AI confirms a confound in fragment-route evaluation: healthy bots cannot initiate a pickup goal for either one nearby fragment or a four-fragment line. Each fragment’s maximum ordinary desirability is 6.25 before travel cost, below the required score greater than 8; the line is never evaluated collectively. Injured bots can pursue fragments, and healthy bots can choose the major. Therefore sparse healthy-bot use of a fragment circuit cannot be attributed to its layout without testing a route-aware collection policy. Do not redesign maps around that AI limitation.

Source geometry now also has route-based exposure profiles: twelve route cases per map for Noon, Raven, Borg 1, Voyager 2 and Temple, using uniform reachable 6×3×6 m cell representatives instead of only authored spawns. These measure potential LOS within the reconstruction, not combat or player probabilities. Raven’s two unresolved clip-separated starts are excluded from its principal reachable field. Temple’s protected starts coexist with a broad exposed overlook near the grenade-launcher location; Voyager 2’s upper gallery also produces local exposure peaks despite more protected circulation elsewhere. Both peaks were inspected in 3D. Compare the sequence of protected approach, exposed control/collection, and departure rather than classifying an entire map by its average visibility.

Source reconstructions establish architectural hypotheses, not observed original Elite Force combat. Original shader/mover semantics and human observation remain limits. Original-layout transfer tests follow below.


### Original-layout transfer exercises

Six original playable blockouts in `research/flow/original-blockouts.mjs` test relationships taken from the references; they do not copy source geometry or enter the production roster. Each contains twelve starts, twelve 5 HP fragments in three lines, four 25 HP recoveries, and exactly one 50 HP major, with the required overheal rules. The counts are experimental controls, not universal inventory recommendations. `prepare-play-exercises.mjs` produces a local native-game entry with map selection, player-height inspection and normal play. Its simulation imports are isolated from ongoing production edits.

| Raven: concealment through rooms and turns; Borg 1: partially overlapping routes at different heights | Cross / Bent Relay | Successive reveals; separate end returns; lower crossing independent of upper interlock; routes rejoin useful fighting space | Neck offsets, screen orientation, chamber proportions, which floor owns the crossing, major position |
| Stock HM_VOY2: recognizable local chambers, upper galleries, meaningful moving access | Triple / Split Transfer | Three distinct room volumes; low offset thresholds; local upper views rather than mapwide sight; a separate service return; physically working lift landing | Room order and proportions, core shape, threshold bends, stair/lift placement, service access |
| Reservoir: symmetric opposing territories around a layered shared contest | Dedicated original transfer exercise still required; the converted reference is playable | Corresponding territories and access costs; interaction between galleries, crossings and lower water routes; visible proximity distinct from arrival time; multiple meaningful ways across the shared space | Territory shape, crossing arrangement, gallery setbacks, lower-loop shape, ascent locations and which approaches can observe or intercept each other |

These are directions for original designs, not evidence of equal fidelity to every source. Relay remains substantially more open than Raven and has only two tiers; it is not a reproduction of Raven’s concealment or Borg 1’s three-tier stack. Ship exercises omit original moving doors. Temple’s remote teleporter excursion, reverse Voyager’s teleport/drop flag circulation, and Reservoir’s water/gallery structure remain distinct lessons; they should not be collapsed into these three templates. Replacing a teleport with a long corridor changes arrival timing and pursuit, even if the abstract graph connects the same places.

### Symmetric territorial layouts are two additional design families

Reservoir and the supplied custom Voyager 2 (`rev_voy2`, a reverse-CTF conversion of CTF_VOY2) have explicit standing in the design process. The custom Voyager layout is distinct from stock `hm_voy2`, which informed the existing ship-interior exercise. Symmetry is part of their spatial organization: corresponding territories let players reuse their understanding of one side while the connections between sides establish the fight. An original variation should preserve that correspondence while inventing new rooms, route geometry and encounter arrangements. Neither family is represented adequately by merely mirroring one of the existing court, relay or ship blockouts.

**Reservoir direction — symmetric layered crossings.** Begin with two corresponding territories and a shared middle whose upper, crossing and lower routes can interact without being interchangeable. Design where opponents can see across layers, where they can reach each other, and where they can intercept a crossing as separate relationships. The lower water circulation changes movement and approach as well as elevation; preserve its tactical role when using water, or explicitly redesign and retest that role if substituting another traversal medium. The gallery-to-major access cost is a reminder to give each layer a purpose beyond looking down at the center. Invent new crossing shapes and territory footprints without retaining the source geometry. Validate territory-to-middle, territory-to-territory, upper-to-lower, escape and interception routes in both directions.

**Custom Voyager 2 direction — symmetric directed circulation.** Begin with corresponding territories linked by access and return cycles rather than assuming all routes are reversible corridors. The supplied ordinary AAS start-to-flag and flag-to-flag routes depend on teleport access, with drops in the return circulation. This makes link placement, arrival orientation, one-way commitment and the point where cycles meet central design variables. Trace an approach, an arrival, a departure/drop and a pursuing or intercepting counter-route from both sides. Special-link timing must be modeled explicitly. Fragony currently has no equivalent teleport system in these exercises; replacing a teleport with stairs or a lift creates a different timing relationship that requires fresh validation. That limitation does not remove this family from the design vocabulary.

For free-for-all adaptations, preserve the usefulness and legibility of both territories without assuming team-base behavior. Keep corresponding route opportunities and resource access, then test whether players actually circulate between them. Match travel time, exposure, ascent/drop cost, entry orientation and interception opportunities across corresponding routes; visual symmetry alone is insufficient. Use paired fragment lines and corresponding 25 HP recovery opportunities where appropriate, while retaining exactly **one shared, contested 50 HP**. Do not mirror that major into two pickups. A fixed point in the layout can hold it, or its approaches can otherwise preserve equivalent opportunities from both sides. Compare traffic and damage by corresponding regions, signed crossing direction, height and route, in addition to mapwide totals. A symmetric map can still funnel almost all fighting into one connection or let one position watch every approach.

These two families are required coverage for the next original-layout transfer pass, alongside the existing three directions. Reservoir's implemented conversion supplies direct engine evidence; custom Voyager's reconstruction and AAS supply structural evidence. Neither substitutes for testing a newly invented symmetric layout. Confidence in those two original-design directions remains less directly tested than the six existing exercises.

The complete native simulation was copied into an isolated research directory because other chats were changing AI, character and impact code during this investigation. All 28 retained matches share the recorded native source hashes. Earlier partially frozen cohorts are excluded. Replaying a recorded seed reproduced its entire event stream and final state exactly; removing the observer reproduced the same final state. Four calibration matches, twelve initial transfer matches, two arcade revisions, six higher-difficulty checks, and four final court-access checks each run six autonomous bots for 90 seconds. Higher-difficulty checks set round-eight AI difficulty after match start; they do not simulate an eight-round purchasing/loadout history. All six final geometries pass 300 directed trips through the native controller, including stairs, drops, recovery approaches and actual lift rides; final geometry hashes accompany those checks.

The final Court, Cross Relay and Triple Transfer each have four cases: two initial-difficulty seeds and two different seeds at higher difficulty. Sampled potential eye-contact occupies approximately 86–89%, 77–86% and 64–66% of living actor time respectively. Median uncensored sight-window lower bounds are 1.0–1.8 s, 0.6–0.8 s and approximately 1.0 s. Major collections overlap opposing damage within the defined three-second window in 8/10, 10/12 and 7/12 collections. These are small bot samples with fixed inventories and a research fragment-circuit policy, not human balance estimates or observations of original Elite Force combat. Geometry and damage/traffic measurements agree that the layouts create different exposure rhythms, although they do not establish that any is a finished good map.

Concrete corrections and reusable failures:

- **A roof is insufficient protection.** The initial court’s ground arcade remained broadly visible. In the matched offset variant, adding opaque interrupted frontages reduced living actor eye-contact fractions from 95.2/94.3% to 88.0/86.5%. The north-arcade field visibility fell from 61.4% to 25.3%, while the upper north remained broadly exposed (66.3% to 65.1%). The major remained contested in all five collections in those two revision matches. This separates a sheltered lower return from the public upper edge without closing the whole court.
- **Visible adjacency is not access.** The first court required about 69 m of planned movement from the major to the middle of either upper rim. Two exposed direct stairs shortened those graph routes to about 37/34 m, alongside the longer outer ascents. Final controller checks pass. Upper traffic did not consistently increase, and first-difficulty recovery use fell; retain this as an access correction, not proof of improved balance.
- **Screens can protect the reward too much.** Bent Relay improved chamber traffic distribution and reduced ground exposure, but major contention was 2/5 collections versus 4/6 in Cross Relay in the initial two seeds. The changed screens and shifted major are bundled, so the test does not identify one isolated cause. More cover is not automatically better contest design.
- **A lift platform must have clear boarding headroom.** An early Cargo landing projected over the lower boarding position. The bot could enter the lift footprint but remained trapped below the landing. Aligning the landing edge to the platform edge corrected actual ascent and exit; graph connectivity alone missed it.
- **Local height is a relationship, not an occupancy quota.** These bots spend only roughly 1–10% of living time above the lower tier. Upper access is physically verified, but that does not establish desirable human control or AI valuation. Check what a gallery sees, its blind space below, its exposed ascent, drop departure and alternative pursuit route separately. Counting all visible lower nodes can even favor the ground because a gallery hides its own undercroft; compare the intended fight/approach, not merely the total count.
- **Protected does not mean uncontested.** The ship service return carries substantial traffic and actual damage in every retained case. It breaks the hub’s sightline but becomes its own corridor fight. Do not label it safe sustain simply because it is behind the main room.

Fragment-agent results are version-specific. The isolated native AI already includes opportunistic fragment detours from concurrent production work, although it still does not initiate a healthy multi-fragment-line goal collectively. The research policy values short connected lines, physical approach cost and collection cost, preserves combat/injury priorities, and restores the native method afterward. In matched Test Works calibration it changed effective fragment gains from 10 to 10 HP and 30 to 40 HP; it did not produce a dramatic general increase. Controlled wall-separation, stronger-major priority and restoration checks pass. Do not carry the earlier individual-pickup limitation forward as a claim that current healthy bots can never collect fragments.

For the next original map, write the intended encounter sequence before drawing rooms: what the arriving player can learn, where commitment exposes them, what interrupts pursuit, and where each exit rejoins. Vary the architectural masses and route order while preserving those relationships. Keep recovery, dominant view and major collection spatially separable. Verify directed physical transitions, actual visibility at eye height, approach cost, departure and counter-route. Treat seed sensitivity, chamber monopolies, unused upper access and unchallenged major collections as questions to investigate, not thresholds to optimize mechanically. Confidence currently supports original blockouts in these architectural directions; human orientation, movement feel and combat choices still require playtesting.
